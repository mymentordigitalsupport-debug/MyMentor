# COURSE: From Addicts to Leaders

## CHAPTER 1: Leadership and Recovery

### Chapter Overview

This chapter establishes the main idea of the course: lasting recovery must grow into leadership. Leadership here does not mean position or title. It means learning to live with truth, responsibility, service, maturity, and steady influence. The learner is invited to move from mere survival into character-based growth.

### What The Learner Will Practice

- Naming the difference between recovery and transformation
- Defining leadership as character and service
- Identifying false ideas of leadership
- Building a personal standard for responsibility and influence

## Lesson 1: Why Recovery Needs Leadership

- `Welcome`: Recovery is not only about what you stop. It is also about who you become.
- `Teaching`: The manuscript argues that treatment alone is not enough. People need direction, purpose, and constructive identity if recovery is going to last.
- `Mentor Note`: A person who cannot lead self well will struggle to rebuild life well.
- `Pause and Reflect`: Where have you thought of recovery only as stopping behavior?
- `Journal`: Write what a healthy, responsible life would look like for you six months from now.
- `Check-In`: How ready are you to grow beyond survival?
- `Action`: Choose one area where you will begin acting with intention this week.
- `Reflection Check`: True or false: lasting recovery is only about abstinence. `False`
- `Complete`: Today you began reframing recovery as a path toward purpose and leadership.

## Lesson 2: Leadership as Character, Not Status

- `Welcome`: Many people hear the word leadership and imagine position, power, or attention.
- `Teaching`: This book defines leadership through truthfulness, humility, integrity, and service. Leadership is about the kind of person you are becoming.
- `Mentor Note`: Title can be given quickly. Character is formed slowly.
- `Pause and Reflect`: Which character traits do you most trust in other people?
- `Journal`: List three traits you want people to experience when they are around you.
- `Check-In`: Which trait feels weakest in your life today: honesty, discipline, humility, or steadiness?
- `Action`: Practice one act of quiet integrity today that no one needs to praise.
- `Reflection Check`: According to this chapter, leadership begins with `character`.
- `Complete`: You have identified leadership as a way of life, not a public image.

## Lesson 3: Service, Responsibility, and Influence

- `Welcome`: Real influence grows when people become dependable.
- `Teaching`: The manuscript ties leadership to service, care, and practical responsibility. A leader carries weight well and helps others flourish.
- `Mentor Note`: Influence without responsibility quickly becomes harm.
- `Pause and Reflect`: Who has influenced your life through steady care rather than loud words?
- `Journal`: Describe one responsibility you have avoided and why it matters.
- `Check-In`: How dependable do you feel right now in daily life?
- `Action`: Finish one neglected task as an act of responsibility.
- `Reflection Check`: Leadership is strongest when it serves `others`.
- `Complete`: Today you connected leadership with trustworthiness and care.

## Lesson 4: What a True Leader Is

- `Welcome`: Healthy leadership has a clear shape.
- `Teaching`: A true leader pursues excellence, fairness, maturity, discipline, and growth in others. Leadership helps build people instead of controlling them.
- `Mentor Note`: Strong people do not need to dominate in order to guide.
- `Pause and Reflect`: What kind of presence makes you feel safe to grow?
- `Journal`: Write a short definition of the leader you want to become.
- `Check-In`: Which quality do you most need to strengthen right now: patience, fairness, courage, or consistency?
- `Action`: Encourage someone else in a sincere and practical way today.
- `Reflection Check`: A true leader helps `develop more leaders`.
- `Complete`: You have started building a healthier picture of leadership.

## Lesson 5: What a True Leader Is Not

- `Welcome`: Clarity also comes from knowing what to reject.
- `Teaching`: The book warns against selfishness, manipulation, pride, deception, and control. These may look powerful, but they damage people and destroy trust.
- `Mentor Note`: Control can imitate strength while hiding insecurity.
- `Pause and Reflect`: Which harmful pattern most easily disguises itself as strength?
- `Journal`: Name one manipulative or self-protective habit you want to leave behind.
- `Check-In`: How honest are you being with yourself about your harmful patterns?
- `Action`: Apologize or correct one small dishonest or controlling behavior if needed.
- `Reflection Check`: True leaders reject `pride and manipulation`.
- `Complete`: Today you named the difference between leadership and domination.

## Lesson 6: Stewardship and Trust

- `Welcome`: Leadership includes caring well for what has been entrusted to you.
- `Teaching`: The manuscript uses stewardship to describe oversight, protection, encouragement, and dignity. Good leadership manages people, time, resources, and responsibility with care.
- `Mentor Note`: Stewardship asks, "What has been placed in my hands, and how will I honor it?"
- `Pause and Reflect`: What in your life currently needs better stewardship?
- `Journal`: Write about one relationship, one habit, and one responsibility you want to manage better.
- `Check-In`: Which area feels most neglected today?
- `Action`: Put one practical system in place for follow-through.
- `Reflection Check`: Stewardship is leadership through `care`.
- `Complete`: You have connected leadership with faithful oversight.

## Lesson 7: Growing Into Mature Leadership

- `Welcome`: Leadership is formed, not rushed.
- `Teaching`: Maturity grows through difficulty, discipline, truth, and ongoing change. The book emphasizes that hardship can train wisdom if a person stays teachable.
- `Mentor Note`: Maturity is not the absence of struggle. It is the way you grow through it.
- `Pause and Reflect`: What challenge in your life has the potential to shape you for good?
- `Journal`: Describe one hardship that could become part of your growth story.
- `Check-In`: How teachable do you feel in this season?
- `Action`: Identify one mentor, practice, or habit that can help you mature.
- `Reflection Check`: Leadership maturity develops over `time`.
- `Complete`: You ended the chapter seeing growth as a disciplined journey.
