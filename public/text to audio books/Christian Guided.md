**Lesson 1:  Gods Original Design for Humanity**

God created humanity in His image, giving every person inherent dignity, worth, and a unique purpose in His creation. Addiction can distort this identity, causing people to see themselves as failures, unworthy, rejected, or defined by their destructive choices. However, your past mistakes and present struggles do not change the value God has placed within you or the purpose for which He created you. Begin to see yourself through God's truth and love rather than through the shame, guilt, and labels attached to your past. Remember that you are God's creation, worthy of love, capable of transformation, and able to rebuild your life with faith, responsibility, and purpose. 

**Lesson 2: Created in the Image of God**

Being created in God's image means that every human being carries inherent dignity, worth, and the ability to reflect God's love, goodness, creativity, and purpose. Addiction can distort a person's understanding of this identity, causing them to see themselves through shame, guilt, broken relationships, and past mistakes rather than through God's love and truth. However, your struggles do not erase your value, and your addiction does not define who God created you to be. You are valuable, deeply loved, and worthy of the opportunity to heal, grow, and rebuild your life. Begin to see yourself through God's eyes rather than through the failures of your past, and recognise that you were created for a meaningful life filled with purpose, hope, and possibility. 

**Lesson 3: The Purpose of Life**

Drug abuse can gradually distract people from their true potential, causing them to lose sight of their inherent gifts, opportunities, relationships, and the purpose that gives life meaning. As addiction takes control, personal growth, meaningful connections, and future possibilities can become neglected or forgotten. Recovery is therefore not simply about stopping drug abuse; it is about rediscovering who you meant to be, recognising your abilities, rebuilding your life, and finding new opportunities to grow. Every person has unique strengths, talents, gifts and potential that can develop your life into meaning and purpose. **See recovery as the beginning of a greater journey of transformation, where overcoming addiction is the first step towards discovering your purpose and building a life worth living.** 

**Lesson 4:  The Garden of Eden**

The Garden of Eden reflects God's original design for humanity, where life was intended to be lived in harmony, order, closeness with Him, and peace within creation. God gave humanity a place of belonging, meaningful relationships, and the responsibility to care for and steward what He had created. This original design teaches us that our lives were never intended to be controlled by chaos, destructive behaviours, or the things that separate us from our true purpose. In recovery, we can begin restoring these principles by developing discipline, rebuilding relationships, taking responsibility for our choices, and strengthening our relationship with God. As your mentor, I encourage you to see recovery as a journey towards restoring order, rediscovering purpose, and experiencing the peace and meaningful life God intended for humanity. 

**Lesson 5: Freedom, Choice and Responsibility**

God created us with the freedom to make choices, but with that freedom it is to choose Gods way.. True freedom is not simply doing whatever we desire; it is having the wisdom and self-control to choose what protects our well-being, values, and purpose in Christ. During recovery, every decision matters because unhealthy choices can reopen the door to destructive behaviours, while wise choices strengthen healing and personal growth. As your mentor, I encourage you to take ownership of your actions, establish healthy boundaries, and surround yourself with influences that support your recovery. Remember, **freedom gives you the ability to choose, but responsibility and wisdom help you protect the life you are rebuilding.** 

**Lesson 6: The First Temptation**

Temptation often begins with deception, presenting something harmful as desirable, harmless, or capable of satisfying a need within us. It works by twisting the truth, making destructive choices appear attractive while hiding the consequences that may follow. When we place our trust in false promises rather than God's wisdom and truth, we become more vulnerable to decisions that can damage our lives. In recovery, it is important to recognise these patterns, question the thoughts that lead us towards destructive behaviours, and develop the discipline to choose what is right rather than what offers temporary satisfaction. As your mentor, I encourage you to remain grounded in God's truth, because recognising deception is an important step towards protecting your freedom and building a life of lasting healing. 

Lesson 7: The Fall of Man

The Fall of the first man created teaches us how disobedience and separation from God introduced shame, fear, guilt, and the desire to hide from the truth about ourselves. With drug addiction, these same emotions can become deeply rooted, causing people to conceal their struggles, withdraw from relationships, and believe they are unworthy of love or forgiveness. However, God's grace does not abandon us in our brokenness; His love continues to pursue us, offering forgiveness, restoration, and the opportunity to begin again. Recovery requires the courage to face the truth, acknowledge our struggles, take responsibility for our actions, and allow God's grace to guide our healing. As your mentor, remember that **you do not have to hide from God because of your past; His grace invites you to step out of shame and move towards a life of truth, healing, and renewed purpose.** 

**Lesson 8: The Consequences of Disobedience**

Destructive choices often create consequences that extend beyond the individual, affecting relationships, families, personal well-being, and the opportunities available to us. Addiction can become a cycle where one harmful decision leads to another, gradually deepening the damage and making it difficult to see a way forward. However, God's grace reminds us that even when our choices have caused pain, our story does not have to end in failure, shame, or regret. Through faith, personal responsibility, forgiveness, and a willingness to change, we can begin repairing what has been damaged and rebuilding our lives. As your mentor, remember that **grace meets you** 

**Lesson 9:  Gods Creation and the Plant Kingdom**

God created the world and everything within it good, giving humanity gifts such as freedom, relationships, opportunities, and the ability to experience joy. These gifts are intended to enrich our lives and help us fulfil and embody our purpose, but when they are misused or placed above God's wisdom, they can become destructive influences. Addiction is an example of how something initially pursued for pleasure, comfort, or escape can gradually become a trap that takes control of a person's choices, relationships, and freedom. In recovery, we must learn to recognise the difference between using God's gifts wisely and allowing our desires to control us. As your mentor, I encourage you to rediscover the goodness in life, exercise wisdom in your choices, and use the gifts you have been given to build a life of freedom, responsibility, and purpose. 

**Lesson 10: Understanding the Origins of Drug Abuse**

Addiction often develops through a cycle of unresolved pain, deception, shame, and the desire to escape emotions or circumstances that feel too difficult to face. A person may initially turn to drugs for comfort, relief, or temporary happiness, but what appears to offer an escape can eventually become another source of suffering. Over time, deception convinces the individual that substances are some form of answer, while shame makes it increasingly difficult to acknowledge the problem and seek help. True recovery must therefore reach deeper than simply the aim to stop drug use; it must address the emotional wounds, harmful beliefs, broken relationships, and spiritual struggles that may contribute to destructive behaviour. As your mentor, I encourage you to find the courage to confront the pain beneath the addiction, allow God's truth and grace to guide your healing, and begin rebuilding your life from the inside out.
